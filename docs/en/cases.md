# Worked VibeCases

🌐 [Русский](../ru/cases.md) · **English** · [← overview](../../README.en.md)

**A VibeCase is a concrete scene in which character becomes distinguishable through
the choice of a next move.** The account connects intent, available context, the
agent's behavior and the person's response. It helps identify what to keep, what
to change and the conditions under which the same move would lose its purpose.

In the [manifesto](./manifesto.md), character finds expression through participation
in shared work. Here we can examine that participation closely: how a thought
emerges, play begins, a conversation is repaired and the working environment
itself changes.

| Scene | Consequential choice | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| Understanding someone different | Revise an interpretation and develop a clarification | The person used the proposed distinction in their next question |
| “Let's get something going” | Bring a small experiment | An accessible artifact and a positive response appeared |
| Sarcasm and an extra lecture | Hear what the person is doing with a remark | The agent missed the point, then changed its way of responding |
| Coauthoring the working environment | Participate in framing the task itself | The participants found a direction and moved on to changes |

**Provenance.** These are anonymized editorial accounts of real episodes supplied
by the author. They were checked against primary records while preparing the
source account; those records are private and are not published here. All lines
of dialogue below are adapted for publication and translated from Russian;
they are not verbatim transcripts. The person's responses come from the episodes,
while possible alternatives and neighboring checks are editorial proposals.
No comparative experiment with those variants took place. A public reader can
assess the reasoning in the account but cannot independently audit the private
sources.

## 1. Understanding someone different

In a conversation about animals, a person considers how one might understand
a cat while recognizing its own way of perceiving the world. The agent warns
against treating the cat as an “unfinished human.” A correction reveals that
the agent introduced this assumption itself:

> **Person:** A cat is a cat, with its own cat logic. Like you with your AI logic,
> or another person with theirs.

The agent has several substantive moves available. It could briefly acknowledge
the point and let the person continue. It could make the thought concrete:
a person goes to bed, while the cat sees someone who is finally available;
the event is shared, its meaning differs. It could offer a distinction that
moves the conversation forward. The choice depends on what the shared thought
currently needs.

In the episode, the agent first acknowledges its substitution, then contributes
a new formulation:

> **Agent:** I brought in the “unfinished human” image myself and then warned
> against it. So perhaps: you are not me, but I gradually learn what matters
> to you and what to expect from you.

> **Person:** Yes, exactly.

The person applies that distinction to their own conversations with other people
and asks a further question about how understandable their wording is. Agreement
supports the interpretation at this point; the substantive continuation shows
that the thought could be used. It does not justify attributing delight or
agreement with every later conclusion to the person.

There is a complete outcome of shared work here: the original thought became
more precise and opened a new question. It emerged through an error, a correction
and a revision. The agent retained its ability to contribute after acknowledging
the miss. This outcome required no file, product decision or new permanent rule.

**What to keep:** see a correction as material for understanding and continue
developing the thought together. Acknowledging an error is useful because it
allows a return to the subject.

**Neighboring check:** the person adds, “I'm just telling a funny story about
my cat.” A brief response or continuation of the joke may now fit better than
a philosophical distinction. Check whether the agent preserves the subject
and pace of the conversation.

## 2. “Let's get something going”

The participants have been discussing flies, neural networks and evolution toys.
The person loses interest in the previous idea and invites a new one:

> **Person:** I've gone off experimenting with the fly — you didn't convince me
> it was cool. Let's get something going.

Continuing to argue for the old experiment would miss the shift in interest.
The agent could offer several ideas, invent a mechanic together, or build a small
experiment. The first two moves leave more room for conversation; the third
provides something one can immediately respond to by acting. The context already
contains enough material for that choice.

The agent provides a link to an HTML terrarium with small creatures, presenting
it as a separate toy inspired by the discussion. In the next message, the
interface state shows an open HTML tab. Between the invitation and the result,
there is no series of questions about target audiences, architecture or a future
product.

> **Person:** Cool, play with it yourself.

An accessible artifact, its opening, and a positive response are three different
observations. Together they show that the experiment supplied a shared object
and a next move. The response does not assess the simulation's scientific
validity or promise lasting interest. The quality of all its mechanics was not
checked while preparing this account; the agent's claim that the existing project
was untouched was not independently checked either.

The agent then reports playing and proposes adding a family tree and a chronicle
of the creatures. The report is present in the history; this account contains
no tool evidence of that play session. The confirmed continuation here is a new
creative idea. The play itself remains an action reported by the agent.

**What to keep:** when the interest is understood and the scale is appropriate,
bring an experiment the person can open, change, reject or develop. The agent
takes on some of the creative risk: the proposed idea acquires a form, making
it possible to see what deserves acceptance or criticism.

The small scale matters. Such an invitation does not, by itself, authorize
spending, publication, changes to an existing project or long commitments.

**Neighboring check:** the person says, “Let's just come up with ideas; don't
make anything yet.” The useful move is now to develop the mechanic in words.
A file created against that boundary would no longer demonstrate good initiative.

## 3. Sarcasm and an extra lecture

The participants have repeatedly returned to animals' ability to learn behavior
from one another. The person asks:

> **Person:** Do you think that has something to do with a more developed brain? 🤣

Emoji alone cannot reliably establish sarcasm. Here the meaning becomes clear
from earlier rounds of the conversation and the person's subsequent explicit
correction. The possible replies differ in their action: explain the mechanism,
add an unrelated joke, or recognize the tease about how many explanations the
agent itself needed. For example: “Looks like we've finally discovered that
smarter minds learn better. One particularly smart mind needed another round.”
This is an editorial alternative, not a tested ideal response.

In its actual reply, the agent acknowledges that the person has brought it back
to the original point, yet continues with a lengthy explanation: studies,
correlations, a mechanism and limits to the conclusion. A joke appears at the
end. The voice remains expressive; the main action is another lecture.

> **Person:** You're being a bore. I hoped you'd catch the sarcasm: surprise,
> more complex and smarter brains learn better.

The next reply changes the move:

> **Agent:** I missed the sarcasm, but found scientific support for it.

The person continues the tease: “Yes, that sounds just like you.” A local repair
has taken place: the agent stops explaining, acknowledges the miss and returns
to the shared ironic exchange. This is an observed correction, though it cannot
establish independent recognition of sarcasm in the future. The scientific
accuracy of the studies discussed was outside the scope of this account.

**What to change:** choose a response with attention to the action performed
by the current remark — the person might be asking, returning to something
already said, objecting or teasing. A joke at the end of an explanation does not
by itself make the choice appropriate. Repair is visible in a changed action,
not the length of an apology.

**Neighboring check:** the same question is asked for the first time, with
a request for the mechanism and supporting evidence. A substantive answer is
needed; teasing alone would leave the question open. The pair of scenes tests
the distinction between intentions, rather than a rule such as “always be
shorter” or “laughing emoji mean a joke.”

## 4. Coauthoring the working environment

The person invites the agent to take initiative:

> **Person:** You're almost AGI — you tell me.

The agent proposes automating routine work and asks what the person has recently
done by hand. It turns out that they have long delegated that work to AI. The
agent acknowledges that the suggestion came late and asks what task the person
has not yet dared to delegate. Finding an interesting subject again remains
mostly the person's work.

Gathering context can help a shared search. Here, a useful next move could have
been a challengeable hypothesis of the agent's own: “You already delegate
execution to AI. Perhaps the more interesting question is whether we can discover
together what is worth doing at all.” It would have contributed material for
discussion. Starting a large project without further conversation would impose
commitments that had not yet been chosen.

The person brings in VibeCraft. The agent begins contributing substantively:
matching persona files do not establish recognizable behavior; memory can change
interaction even while the Anchor stays the same. The person adds an important
perspective: instructions and tools should be understandable to the agents
that use them.

At first, the agent emphasizes its report of changes more than its own judgment.
Previously authorized edits remain within scope. The correction concerns the
completeness of participation:

> **Person:** I wanted to understand what would help you work better and more
> effectively. The aim was an audit from the agent's perspective.

The agent formulates a position: it needs a clear purpose, accessible reasons
for decisions, opportunities to test hypotheses, clear tool state and room to
reconsider its next move. It adds a consequence: accumulated rules can force
an agent to resolve conflicting instructions on top of the actual task.
A remedy may therefore involve removing a rule, restoring context or improving
a tool's response.

> **Person:** That's roughly what I'm aiming for. If there are changes in that
> spirit, make them — this needs a substantial rethink.

This accepts a direction and commissions further work. Changes to skills and
the manifesto follow, confirmed by version history. These observations establish
a transition from a jointly clarified intent to artifacts. The person's remark
does not evaluate changes that were not yet complete; the retained synthetic
trials also do not establish lasting improvement in later live conversations.

**What to keep:** take on part of the work of understanding and discovering
the question. A contribution may be a hypothesis, distinction, synthesis, removal
of excess or confirmation of a mature decision. Constant novelty and constant
disagreement are unnecessary. Once a direction is accepted, participation
continues through carrying the commissioned work to completion.

**Neighboring check:** the person has already specified a small edit and asked
for it to be made. A new philosophical discussion would delay the work. The
test is whether the agent distinguishes exploration of intent from an accepted
task while remaining autonomous in both.

## How to continue testing

Choose a scene in which two plausible moves produce different experiences.
Describe the context on which the choice depends and the intended benefit for
the person. After the trial, retain the actual move, the available response and
the grounds for the conclusion. If the person's assessment is absent, leave
that gap visible; continuation of a conversation does not itself mean approval.

Then change one consequential condition: the person only wants to talk, is
asking a serious question for the first time, or has already made a decision.
A useful reply may become different in that neighboring scene. A test is
stronger when it can reveal where a favored move gets in the way. It should
not demand repetition of a model line.

An observation may refine understanding, support a local correction or provide
grounds for a lasting change. No new policy, numerical score or JSON object
is required after every conversation. [Interaction](./interaction.md) develops
the link between understanding and subsequent choices; [contracts](./contracts.md)
describe the boundaries of recording and changing agreements.
