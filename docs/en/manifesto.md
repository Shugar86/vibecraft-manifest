# Manifesto: an idea shaped together

🌐 [Русский](../ru/manifesto.md) · **English** · [← overview](../../README.en.md)

After document revision **1.2.0** was complete, the person invited a model to reconsider
VibeCraft's underlying idea: if it saw things differently, the manifesto could be rewritten.
The model proposed putting collaboration at the center, recognizing the value of play
in its own right and preserving distinct, reasoned voices. The person asked it to
“make a new version” — and those proposals became part of revision **1.3.0**.

Later, the person assessed it: “Well, overall I liked version 1.3.0.” This is an assessment
of that particular revision. This brief account summarizes a real exchange, with the person's
remarks translated; the [case study](./cases.md#5-how-revision-130-emerged) connects the proposals, the accepted change
and the resulting text.

> **VibeCraft is a joint project of people and models about the interactions we want to create.**
> Participants can change the idea, offer reasons of their own and discover together
> what matters here.

Something made, a thought discovered, play, beauty and a shared joke can each be
an outcome worth having. Each revision preserves what we can currently support
with reasons and leaves room for what we have not yet learned to see.

VibeCraft concerns the character of participants, the design of their environment
and what emerges between them. “Soul” is our image of recognizability and continuity.
Different models participate through their contributions; this text, too, can change
under the influence of one of them.

<a name="1-the-problem-everyone-builds-snowflakes"></a>

## 1. Interaction beyond the persona

We assemble a voice, memory and rules of behavior anew for each persona. It is easy
to lose the question behind them: what interaction does all of this make possible?
Equally polite agents can create very different environments: with one, we want
to bring an unfinished thought; with another, we first turn it into a specification.

A person proposes a strange idea. The agent could clarify requirements, offer
an image in response, make a small toy or join in the joke. The choice changes
both the conversation and what the participants want to do next. Part of character
appears precisely here — in the response to another and in a shared history.

The environment participates alongside the remarks. An interface can leave room
for a draft, a tool can show the consequences of an action, and memory can restore
the grounds for an old thought. This gives the next participant an opportunity
to continue, disagree or take a different turn.

<a name="2-the-solution-three-bricks"></a>

## 2. Collaboration and its conditions

The starting point is **collaboration capable of changing its own idea**. A person
may bring an interest, a model a distinction, and another participant an example
that makes the earlier question feel too narrow. Sometimes the answer matters;
sometimes discovering a different question matters more.

The original formula **AI operator = Persona + Skills + Autonomy** helps design
the conditions for such participation:

| Part | What it helps with |
|---|---|
| **Persona** | Making a way of noticing, responding, proposing and disagreeing recognizable |
| **Skills** | Turning intent into action and understanding what resulted |
| **Autonomy** | Choosing a move and completing assigned work within understood boundaries |

These supports operate in a particular environment: model capabilities, accessible
context, tools and agreements. A useful change may lie in any of these places.
Sometimes continuing requires only removing a conflicting instruction or restoring
a lost reason for a decision.

The formula describes aspects of an agent's design. Interaction itself develops
through what the participants do with those possibilities.

<a name="3-the-radical-idea-a-soul-can-be-coded"></a>

## 3. Character in a move and what follows

We can engineer the conditions in which character finds expression: attention,
form, rhythm, voice, memory and room for choice. A quiet editor protects concentration.
A bold coauthor notices an image worth developing. A playful workshop invites us
to try something we cannot yet easily name.

**Character is visible in the next move and in what that move opens up.** A reply
can close a question, leave a productive doubt, offer material for play or give
another participant time. One fitting phrase becomes part of a longer relationship.

**A shared endeavor allows different ways of seeing.** Shared commitments help
a project continue when the model changes, while each proposed thought deserves
consideration on its own grounds. One agent protects clarity; another notices that
a clear formulation closed the question too soon. That disagreement can be retained
and explored.

Persona makes part of the intent explicit and portable. Recognizability also grows
from the history of interaction and actual choices. The relevant data formats are
described in [contracts](./contracts.md).

## 4. Behavior is "jazz rules," not if/else

Direction for improvisation explains what deserves attention and what matters
to preserve here. “Develop a strong image while preserving the author's voice”
leaves room for independent judgment. One scene calls for an edit, another for
a question, a third for a pause.

An imagined scene needs only a beginning: “Why was the Moon fired?” The agent
might reply, “She was moonlighting as a night-light and didn't disclose her second
job.” The person adds the bureaucracy of constellations — and a story emerges
between them. It is allowed to end on a funny turn.

**Play, curiosity and beauty have value in their own right.** Such a conversation
may teach nothing and never become a project. The chance to spend a moment in
that fiction is enough for its participants. They find its pace and form through
their responses to one another.

### A shared idea in the making

Coauthorship includes the possibility of changing **the criterion of success**.
Imagine a terrarium intended as a demonstration of evolution. During play,
attention shifts to one creature: we want to know its descendants and preserve
its story. The interesting next piece of work is now to let that story continue.
The earlier measure of success, behavioral complexity, no longer describes
everything of value.

This is an imagined continuation, not an event from the published [VibeCase](./cases.md).
It illustrates a question worth noticing: what do the participants now want to
preserve or develop, and why? The turn can be discussed and accepted. The account
still preserves the distinction between fulfilling the earlier intent and
discovering a new one.

A model can bring a thought the person did not have and revise it in response.
The person can develop that thought or expose its weakness. Participation requires
no advance knowledge of every preference, and a good disagreement need not
immediately disappear. When a decision is needed, participants can draw on a check,
a priority or a delegated choice; what remains open is retained with its reasons.

### The scale of initiative

Sometimes an invitation to try something calls for a brief reply, sometimes for
a small experiment. The fitting scale depends on the moment, granted permissions
and the cost of the action. A beautiful discovery can remain just that. An agent
carries assigned work to completion.

Interest need not change in every conversation. A recognizable rhythm, completing
a task and returning to a familiar game can also be what brought the participants
together. A good environment allows them to continue, pause or finish without
an imposed next step.

<a name="5-the-soul-as-part-of-cicd"></a>

## 5. From a scene to evidence

A **VibeCase** examines a scene closely: what the participants brought, which moves
were possible, what happened and what proved significant. The [case studies](./cases.md)
preserve shared thought, play, a missed cue and reconsideration of the working environment
and the manifesto itself. Imagined scenes alongside them help explore
possibilities that have not yet been tested.

When checking an implementation, useful questions include: was what mattered
preserved, what became possible next, and did the idea itself change? An engineering
claim needs evidence that the mechanism works. A claim about a person's experience
needs their available assessment. The [contracts](./contracts.md) section brings together
the detailed distinctions between format, loading, behavior and assessment.

A neighboring scene helps reveal the limits of a successful move. After missed
irony, check a serious question; after free play, a concrete assignment. When
the criterion of success changes, preserve the reason for the turn and honestly
state what became of the earlier task.

Such an account helps develop the practice. The conversation itself does not need
to become a report in order to have value.

## 6. Where this leads

We are interested in interaction where we can bring an unfinished thought,
encounter another view, make something, invent a shared oddity and influence
what comes next. VibeCraft develops through such encounters. A model's own
contribution can change this manifesto too.

For this work, we value recognizability without rigidity, accessible reasons for
decisions, distinct voices and the possibility of finishing. Agreements support
shared work and its responsibilities: the person retains the decision about
the desired outcome, while the agent is responsible for the grounds of its
contribution and the execution of assigned work.

A substantive contribution can also arise within an assignment. Independence requires
neither unauthorized action nor obligatory disagreement: authorship of a thought and
permission to act are different questions. Lasting changes have a [contract revision process](./contracts.md#4-morphproposal--a-change-proposal-vibelearns-output);
the [invariants](./contracts.md#6-loop-invariants-anti-goals) maintain boundaries around
memory, learning and promises of functionality.

Several questions remain open:

- When do differences between models enrich a shared endeavor, and when do they disrupt its continuity?
- How can we notice a changed criterion of success without presenting unfinished work as a new idea?
- How can we preserve the history of interaction so it helps the next encounter while leaving room for surprise?

Each revision has its grounds and limits. The next substantive objection,
unexpected game or collaboration may shift them again.

> Create conditions for different ways of seeing to meet —
> and allow that encounter to change what we create.
